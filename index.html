02 · The Gap

Where contract revenue breaks

  • Invoice ingestion already works. RevRec accepts flat invoice and CSV data. What's missing is contract ingestion: taking in a contract change and updating the revenue schedule automatically.
  • Contract changes are where errors start. Seat changes, pauses, cancellations, and multi-product deals don't resolve from a flat import. Errors build quietly until close.
  • The problem reaches the CFO. A finance team that misses close loses credibility with the board, whichever billing system caused it.

B2B SaaS with its own billing

Billing runs through Salesforce, NetSuite, or a homegrown system. Deals that bundle license, services, and usage are where manual allocation fails.

Revenue from several systems

Stripe, a marketplace, and an ERP at once. A RevRec tied to one billing system sees only part of the revenue.

03 · Landscape

Five competitor strategies

Zuora: the deepest engine

The enterprise standard for complex contracts, with a decade's head start.

Stripe: the cheap add-on

0.25% of recognized revenue. Works from invoices, not contracts.

Chargebee: pricing complexity

Usage, credits, and ramps create recognition work. RevRec is one of seven modules.

Maxio: the narrow specialist

Built for $1M–$100M ARR B2B software. Proof that a narrow focus can win.

Unbundlers: RightRev, Leapfin, LedgerUp, Ordway

Plug into any billing and combine all revenue in one subledger. The biggest threat.

Also watch. NetSuite and Sage Intacct are making built-in RevRec good enough for simple contracts. Stripe will move upmarket when it can.

Where the wedge sits

Contract complexity vs. dependence on one billing system

Open ground: mid-complexity contracts, any billing source, strong on contract changes.
04 · The Wedge

Win on contract changes, not depth

  • Don't fight Zuora on allocation. Being a decade behind on complex price-splitting (SSP) edge cases is a losing fight.
  • Recurly already does the hard part. It updates revenue schedules for lifecycle events millions of times a day for consumer subscriptions. Unbundlers struggle here.
  • Competitors see the same problem. LedgerUp says most deferred-revenue errors come from mismatched contract and billing data, not recognition logic.
Contract eventWhat breaks todayThe lifecycle-native hypothesis
3-year deal license + services + usagePrice split across three streams by handContract ingested once; schedule generated and versioned
Mid-term seat changeManual re-allocation, hard to auditHandled as a lifecycle event, like a $9.99 subscription change
Renewal with new pricingOld and new schedules reconciled by handRenewal links to the prior schedule with a full audit trail
The bet is that finance doesn't need a smarter allocation engine. It needs every contract change tracked as an event, not a spreadsheet edit. Section 05 tests that.
05 · Customer Discovery

20 interviews before any code

Everything above is a hypothesis. This is how I'd test it.

WhoWhat they can tell me
Revenue accountantWhich contract changes force manual entries, and how often
ControllerClose timeline, team size, existing budget
CFOWhat triggers a purchase and what it competes with
RevOpsWhether contract feeds can be built against their systems
External auditorWhat evidence they'll accept

Sample: 6 still manual, 5 on a point tool, 4 outgrowing Stripe, 3 unhappy on Zuora, 2 who chose a competitor over Recurly.

What I'm listening for
  • Proof, not opinions. Screen-share the close checklist and workbook.
  • Existing spend. Contractors, advisors, or tools already paid for.
  • Trigger events. Audit findings, IPO prep, a new CFO, an ERP migration.
The key question. Ask accountants to rank their manual work. If fewer than 12 of 20 put contract-change propagation first, the wedge is wrong and the build shifts toward allocation.
Kill criteria
  • No one already pays to solve this.
  • Purchases don't cluster around trigger events.
  • RevOps says they can build and maintain contract-lifecycle feeds in-house, and the real problem is judgment.
06 · Pricing

Set the price before the product

The biggest risk is pricing too low. Copying Stripe's 0.25% would undervalue a product that replaces headcount and audit risk.

CapabilityEffect on price
Contract ingestion from any sourceLeader
Automatic schedule updates on contract changeLeader
Audit trail an auditor can followLeader
Multi-entity, multi-currencyLeader
Dashboards, ERP connectorsFiller expected, not deciding
Charging extra for the audit trail, per-seat pricing, paid migrationKiller
  • Model: a platform fee based on entities, plus basis points on recognized revenue, priced above Stripe.
  • Validate first: Van Westendorp and conjoint studies before setting the number.
07 · Build vs. Partner

Don't build a second Zuora

LayerCall
Contract ingestion and event modelOwn
Workflow and audit trailOwn
Allocation logicPartner RightRev, Leapfin, or Trullion
ERP connectors and taxPartner Celigo, Avalara
Precondition. A public ingestion API, so any system can send contracts without knowing Recurly's internals.

In v1

Ingestion API and CSV import, automatic schedules, ASC 606 / IFRS 15 patterns, multi-entity, audit trail, NetSuite and Intacct export.

Not in v1

Leases, built-in allocation logic, AI assist until evals pass, self-serve signup, SAP and Oracle.

Migration: free, 6–10 weeks. Run in parallel against three closed months, get the opening balance signed, then switch at a quarter boundary.

08 · Applied AI

The model flags. The controller decides.

Revenue recognition has a right answer, and mistakes mean restatements. AI finds issues and records reasoning. People make the calls.

Onboarding

Reads contracts, cites the relevant standard, and proposes a setup for approval.

Exception queue

Flags missed changes each close, ranked by dollar impact.

Audit log

Stores each decision with citation and reviewer, so audit answers take seconds.

  • Evals first. Test on design-partner contracts. The release metric is missed exceptions, since a miss costs far more than a false flag.
  • Math stays deterministic. The engine calculates schedules. The model never does.
  • No source, no answer. If nothing relevant is found, route to a human.
09 · Go-to-Market and Risks

Start mid-market

Customer

$10M–$500M ARR B2B SaaS with multi-product contracts, reconciling by hand or on a point tool.

Entry

5–8 design partners with free pilots on real historical contracts.

Channel

Existing Recurly enterprise relationships.

Packaging

Sold standalone, not tied to Recurly Subscriptions.

Main risks
  • Unbundlers add lifecycle handling. Our narrowest moat, so speed matters.
  • Stripe adds contract ingestion. Target finance buyers outside Stripe billing.
  • Pricing too low. Easier to discount later than to raise.
  • Compliance timing. SOC 1 Type II takes months. Start early.

Holding firm

Recurly's lifecycle-event engine is a real asset the unbundlers lack. Whether buyers value it most is what the week-6 gate tests.

Open to evidence

If allocation is the real blocker, the build order flips.

10 · Measurement

What I'd track

  • Customer: schedule accuracy, days to close, time to go live.
  • Product: share of changes with no manual entry, AI override rate.
  • Business: realized price vs. list, win rate vs. unbundlers, implementation days.
  • Compliance: audit findings and restatements. These lag, so watch exception and override trends.
